Home Emergency Checklist

Know exactly what to do in the first critical minutes of any home emergency. Speed and correct action minimize damage and save lives.

Updated April 2026 10 min read Print-friendly version
Life-threatening emergency? Call 911 first. These checklists assume you and everyone in the home are safe. Always prioritize human life over property.

The actions you take in the first 15-60 minutes of a home emergency determine how much damage you sustain. Below are emergency-specific checklists for every major home disaster.

Burst or Leaking Pipe

ACT WITHIN MINUTES

Water damage spreads fast -- 1 inch of standing water can destroy flooring, drywall, insulation, and cause mold within 24-48 hours. Every minute counts.

  • 1
    Shut off the main water supply immediatelyMain shutoff is typically at the water meter (street side), under the kitchen sink, or in basement/utility room. Turn clockwise to close.
  • 2
    Turn off electricity in affected areasIf water is near outlets or electrical panels, flip the breaker for those zones. Do NOT enter standing water if it could be electrified.
  • 3
    Open faucets to drain remaining waterOpen all faucets on the lowest floor to drain water still in pipes after main shutoff.
  • 4
    Document damage with photos/videoRecord everything BEFORE moving furniture or starting cleanup. Capture the broken pipe, standing water, wet walls, damaged items.
  • 5
    Remove standing water ASAPUse towels, mops, buckets, or a wet/dry vacuum. Move furniture to dry areas. Remove soaked rugs (mold starts in 24 hours).
  • 6
    Call a licensed emergency plumberYou need professional repair before restoring water service. Find emergency plumbers at HomeServiceDirectory.
  • 7
    Call a water damage restoration companyProfessional drying equipment (dehumidifiers, air movers) is needed within the first 24 hours to prevent mold.
  • 8
    Call your insurance companyBurst pipes from sudden events are typically covered. File your claim within 24-48 hours.

Flooding (Basement or Storm)

HIGH HAZARD

Floodwater -- from outside or a sewer backup -- is a Category 3 (black water) hazard containing bacteria, sewage, and pathogens. Do NOT enter without proper protection.

Do NOT enter flooded areas if: Water level is unknown, electrical hazards are present, floodwater source is sewage/sewer, structural damage is suspected.
  • 1
    Do NOT enter if water may be electrifiedSubmerged outlets, wiring, or appliances can electrify standing water. Call utility company to disconnect power from outside.
  • 2
    Shut off main water and electricity from outsideUse the exterior shutoff and breaker panel if accessible without entering flooded area.
  • 3
    Document from doorway before enteringTake photos and video of water level and damage from a safe vantage point.
  • 4
    Wear PPE if you must enterRubber boots, rubber gloves, N95 mask minimum. Sewage water requires full hazmat precautions.
  • 5
    Call a water damage restoration companyTruck-mounted extraction equipment removes water far faster than any DIY method. Call within hours -- not days.
  • 6
    Do NOT use a household vacuum for waterNever use regular vacuums or fans inside before professional extraction -- spreads contamination and can cause electrical hazards.
  • 7
    Contact FEMA if disaster-declared floodingVisit DisasterAssistance.gov. Note: standard homeowners insurance does NOT cover flood. Separate NFIP flood policy required.

Fire or Smoke Damage

CALL 911 FIRST

After the fire is out and firefighters have cleared the scene, the clock starts on a long restoration process. Smoke and soot continue to cause damage for hours and days after the fire.

  • 1
    CALL 911 -- evacuate immediatelyGet everyone out. Don't attempt to fight a fire that's already spreading. Meet at your pre-designated meeting point.
  • 2
    Wait for fire department all-clear before re-enteringNever re-enter until firefighters confirm structural safety. Smoke inhalation and CO are invisible killers.
  • 3
    Document the damage (only once cleared)Photograph every room -- smoke damage often affects areas far from the fire's origin. Document HVAC vents, attic spaces, closets.
  • 4
    Do NOT clean soot yourselfImproper cleaning spreads soot further into surfaces. Professional ozone treatment and proper soot removal is required.
  • 5
    Call your insurance company immediatelyFire claims are among the most comprehensive -- dwelling, contents, additional living expenses (ALE) all typically covered.
  • 6
    Secure the propertyBoard up windows, tarp the roof if needed. Insurers require you to prevent additional damage.
  • 7
    Call a fire/smoke restoration companyIICRC-certified fire restoration pros have specialized equipment for soot removal, deodorization, and structural assessment.

Gas Leak

EVACUATE IMMEDIATELY
Signs of a gas leak: Rotten egg / sulfur smell, hissing sound near gas line, dead vegetation above underground line, bubbling in standing water, dizziness/nausea indoors.
  • 1
    Leave immediately -- do not turn on/off any switchesAny spark from a light switch, phone, or appliance can ignite gas. Leave doors open as you exit. Don't waste time grabbing belongings.
  • 2
    Call 911 and your gas company from outsideCall from your cell phone once you're at least 100 feet from the building. Never use a landline inside.
  • 3
    Do NOT re-enter until clearedGas company technicians must confirm the leak is fixed and the structure is safe before anyone enters.
  • 4
    Call a licensed plumber or gas fitterGas line repair requires a licensed professional. Do not attempt DIY gas repairs under any circumstances.

Mold Discovery

DO NOT DISTURB

Discovering mold -- whether visible growth or a persistent musty smell -- requires a careful, measured response. Disturbing mold can release millions of spores into your air.

  • 1
    Do NOT disturb, spray, or scrub moldTouching or disturbing mold releases spores. Do not use bleach spray on visible mold before assessment.
  • 2
    Close off the affected areaSeal doors, close HVAC vents in the affected room to prevent spore spread throughout the home.
  • 3
    Identify and stop the moisture sourceMold needs moisture to grow. Look for leaks, high humidity, condensation. Fix the source or mold will return.
  • 4
    Document with photos -- don't touchPhotograph visible growth, water stains, and affected areas. Note any musty odors and their location.
  • 5
    Get a certified mold inspectionA certified industrial hygienist (CIH) or IICRC-certified mold inspector can assess extent and type of contamination.
  • 6
    Check insurance coverageIf mold resulted from a covered water event (burst pipe), your policy may cover remediation. Document the moisture source carefully.
  • 7
    Hire IICRC-certified remediators onlyImproper mold removal can spread contamination. Certified professionals follow strict containment and disposal protocols.

Storm or Wind Damage

DOCUMENT FAST

After a major storm, insurance companies are flooded with claims. The sooner you document and file, the better your position. Water entering through roof damage can cause secondary damage within hours.

  • 1
    Wait for storm to fully passDo not go outside or inspect roof during lightning, high winds, or before the storm has completely passed.
  • 2
    Document exterior damage immediatelyPhotograph roof, siding, windows, fencing, gutters. Note time and date. Pull a storm report from NWS to confirm the event.
  • 3
    Prevent further damage (mitigation)Place buckets under active leaks. Tarp damaged roof sections. Board up broken windows. Save all receipts -- insurance may reimburse emergency mitigation.
  • 4
    Do NOT climb on roof without a professionalStorm damage can compromise structural integrity. Have a licensed roofer assess before any DIY work.
  • 5
    File insurance claim within 24-48 hoursStorm claims are time-sensitive. Report promptly and get your claim number assigned.
  • 6
    Beware of storm chasers / door-knockersAfter major storms, unlicensed contractors swarm neighborhoods. Verify licenses before signing anything. Use HomeServiceDirectory for verified local pros.

Extended Power Outage

GENERATOR SAFETY CRITICAL
  • 1
    Report outage to your utility companyUse their outage map to confirm scope. Keep your account number accessible.
  • 2
    NEVER run a generator indoors or in garageCarbon monoxide kills within minutes. Generators must be placed at least 20 feet from any door, window, or vent.
  • 3
    Protect appliances from surgeTurn off and unplug major appliances. When power returns, let the grid stabilize 5 minutes before reconnecting.
  • 4
    Food safety after 4+ hoursRefrigerator keeps food safe about 4 hours if unopened. Freezer: 48 hours full, 24 hours half-full. When in doubt, throw it out.
  • 5
    Inspect for electrical damage after outage endsIf outage was storm-related, have a licensed electrician inspect your panel before fully resuming normal use.

Sewer or Drain Backup

BIOHAZARD

Sewage backup is a Category 3 (black water) biohazard. It contains bacteria, viruses, and pathogens. This is NOT a DIY cleanup -- professional biohazard remediation is required.

Do NOT attempt to clean sewage backup yourself. Exposure can cause serious illness. Standard homeowners insurance often does NOT cover sewer backup unless you added the endorsement.
  • 1
    Stop using all water in the homeDo not flush toilets, run sinks, or use appliances that drain. This worsens backup.
  • 2
    Do NOT enter backed-up areaSewage water contains E. coli, hepatitis, and other serious pathogens. Stay out completely.
  • 3
    Document from a safe distancePhotograph and video the affected area without entering or touching anything.
  • 4
    Call a licensed plumber immediatelyDetermine if the blockage is in your home's lateral line or the city main. City issues may be their responsibility.
  • 5
    Call a biohazard/sewage cleanup companyCertified remediation companies have proper PPE, containment protocols, and disposal methods for sewage material.
  • 6
    Check your insurance policy for sewer backup riderStandard policies often exclude this. If you have the endorsement, file your claim immediately.
