🏠 General Home Service Questions

What should I do in a home emergency?

In any home emergency, follow these steps:

  1. Ensure personal safety first. If there is fire, gas, or electrical danger, evacuate and call 911.
  2. Stop the source. For water: turn off the main water shutoff. For gas: turn off the gas shutoff and leave the building. For electrical: trip the circuit breaker.
  3. Document everything. Take photos and video before any cleanup — this is critical for insurance claims.
  4. Call a professional immediately. For water damage, mold, fire, or sewage — every hour matters. Use HomeServiceDirectory.com to find a licensed 24/7 provider in your city.
  5. Call your insurance company. File a claim as soon as possible. Ask about emergency accommodation coverage if your home is uninhabitable.
How do I choose the right home service contractor?

Follow this checklist when hiring any home service contractor:

  • License: Verify with your state licensing board. Never hire an unlicensed contractor.
  • Insurance: Require proof of general liability AND workers' compensation insurance. If a worker is injured on your property without workers' comp, you may be liable.
  • Reviews: Check Google, Yelp, and BBB. Look for patterns in negative reviews, not just star ratings.
  • Written estimate: Never start work without a signed written contract with price, scope, timeline, and warranty terms.
  • No full upfront payment: Reputable contractors never require 100% payment before work begins. A 20–30% deposit is standard.
  • Years in business: Longer track record reduces risk. Verify they've been in business at the address they claim.

What home emergencies require immediate 24/7 response?

These home emergencies require same-day or within-hours professional response:

  • Burst or frozen pipe — water damage compounds rapidly by the hour
  • Active flooding or sewage backup — health hazard, structural risk
  • Fire or smoke damage — structural stability may be compromised
  • Gas leak — call your gas company AND a licensed plumber/HVAC tech
  • No heat in freezing temperatures — pipes at risk, health danger
  • Electrical hazard — sparking outlets, burning smell, water near panels
  • Storm roof damage — emergency tarping prevents interior water damage
  • Lockout — emergency locksmith available 24/7 in most cities

💧 Water Damage Questions

What should I do immediately after water damage?
  1. Stop the water source — turn off the main water shutoff valve
  2. Cut electricity to affected areas from the breaker panel
  3. Document everything — photos and video before any cleanup
  4. Remove standing water with a wet-dry vacuum or mop
  5. Move valuables off wet floors; lift carpet edges
  6. Call a restoration company within 24 hours — mold starts in 24–72 hours
  7. Call your insurance company and open a claim
  8. Keep receipts for all emergency expenses (hotel, meals, temporary repairs)
How long does water damage restoration take?

The full water damage restoration timeline depends on severity:

  • Water extraction: Same day, 1–4 hours
  • Drying (Class 1–2 damage): 3–5 days with commercial drying equipment
  • Drying (Class 3–4 / severe): 5–14 days
  • Mold prevention treatment: Applied during drying phase
  • Reconstruction (drywall, flooring): 1–4 weeks after drying complete

Total timeline from emergency to fully restored: 2–6 weeks for moderate damage. The restoration company handles the drying phase; reconstruction may require a separate general contractor.

What is IICRC certification and why does it matter?

IICRC stands for the Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification — the industry's gold standard certification body. IICRC certification means a technician has completed standardized training in water damage restoration (WRT), applied structural drying (ASD), or other specialties.

Why it matters: IICRC-certified technicians follow established protocols for moisture measurement, drying calculations, and documentation. This protects you from over-drying (unnecessarily extended rental of equipment) and under-drying (mold growth from inadequate drying). Insurance companies recognize IICRC documentation. Always ask for IICRC certification numbers — they can be verified at iicrc.org.

🔧 Plumbing Questions

How do I shut off water to my house?

Every homeowner should know the location of their main water shutoff valve before an emergency occurs. Common locations:

  • Basement or crawl space: On the wall where the water main enters from outside, usually near the front of the house
  • Garage: On an interior wall, near the water heater
  • Utility closet: Near the water heater or mechanical equipment
  • Outside: Near the curb (requires a water meter key to operate)

Gate valves (round wheel) turn clockwise to close. Ball valves (lever handle) turn 90 degrees to close (perpendicular to pipe = off). Test your shutoff valve once a year so it doesn't seize.

What are signs of a hidden water leak?
  • Water bill suddenly increases without explanation
  • Sound of running water when no fixtures are in use
  • Warm or wet spots on floors (slab leak)
  • Water meter continues moving after all water is turned off
  • Musty smell or visible mold without an obvious source
  • Staining, warping, or soft spots on walls, ceilings, or floors
  • Low water pressure without an obvious cause

To confirm a leak: turn off all water in the home. Check your water meter. If the dial is still moving, you have a leak somewhere in the system. Call a licensed plumber for leak detection services.

❄️ HVAC Questions

Why is my AC not cooling my house?

Common reasons an AC runs but doesn't cool properly:

  • Dirty air filter: Restricts airflow — replace every 1–3 months
  • Low refrigerant: Leak in the system — must be diagnosed and recharged by a licensed technician
  • Frozen evaporator coil: Usually caused by low refrigerant or airflow restriction
  • Dirty condenser coils: Reduces heat dissipation — needs professional cleaning
  • Failing compressor: Most expensive component — may indicate time for replacement
  • Undersized system: If the AC was always undersized for your square footage, it will struggle in extreme heat
  • Ductwork leaks: Conditioned air escaping before reaching rooms

Always check and replace the air filter first — it's free and fixes 20% of AC problems.

How often should HVAC be serviced?

Recommended schedule:

  • Air filter: Every 1–3 months (monthly in high-use or pet households)
  • AC tune-up: Once per year, ideally in spring before cooling season
  • Furnace tune-up: Once per year, ideally in fall before heating season
  • Duct cleaning: Every 3–5 years, or after renovation, mold discovery, or pest infestation
  • Full system inspection: Every 2–3 years for systems under 10 years old; annually for older systems

Regular maintenance extends equipment life by 5–10 years and prevents costly emergency breakdowns.

🦠 Mold Questions

Is black mold dangerous?

Stachybotrys chartarum (commonly called "black mold") can produce mycotoxins that are harmful to humans, particularly those with respiratory conditions, allergies, or compromised immune systems. Common symptoms from mold exposure include coughing, sneezing, nasal congestion, skin and eye irritation, and in severe cases, respiratory illness.

However, the color of mold does not determine its toxicity — many mold types appear black or dark green. The key facts: (1) ANY mold growth in a home should be addressed. (2) Mold testing is the only way to identify species. (3) Areas under 10 square feet can be DIY-remediated with proper protection; larger areas require a professional. (4) Bleach does not permanently kill mold on porous surfaces — it removes the appearance but not the root structure.

How do I prevent mold after water damage?

Mold begins growing within 24–72 hours of moisture exposure. Prevention steps:

  1. Begin water extraction immediately
  2. Run commercial dehumidifiers and air movers — consumer fans are not sufficient
  3. Remove wet drywall, carpet, and insulation within 24–48 hours
  4. Apply EPA-registered antimicrobial spray to affected surfaces
  5. Monitor moisture readings with a professional moisture meter
  6. Keep indoor humidity below 60% during drying
  7. Do not replace drywall or flooring until moisture readings return to baseline

What are signs I need a new roof?
  • Shingles are curling, buckling, or missing
  • Granules accumulating in gutters or downspout areas
  • Daylight visible through roof boards in the attic
  • Sagging areas on the roof deck
  • Active leaks or water stains on ceilings
  • Roof is more than 20 years old (asphalt) or 50 years old (tile)
  • More than 30% of the shingles are damaged

Get a free inspection from a licensed roofer after any significant storm. If your roof is older than 15–20 years and you have storm damage, it may be more cost-effective to replace than repair.

⚡ Electrical Questions

What are signs of dangerous electrical problems?

Call a licensed electrician immediately if you notice:

  • Burning smell from outlets, switches, or electrical panel
  • Outlets or switches that are warm to the touch
  • Sparks when plugging in appliances
  • Flickering lights throughout the home (not just one fixture)
  • Circuit breakers that trip repeatedly
  • Buzzing or crackling sounds from the electrical panel
  • Discolored or charred outlets
  • Shocks when touching outlets or switches

These are signs of potentially dangerous wiring issues. Do not use extension cords as a long-term solution for overloaded circuits — add dedicated circuits instead.

🏗️ Foundation Repair Questions

What are signs of foundation problems?
  • Horizontal cracks in basement walls (most serious — indicates lateral pressure)
  • Stair-step cracks in brick or block exterior walls
  • Doors and windows that stick or no longer close squarely
  • Visible gaps between walls and ceiling, or walls and floor
  • Floors that slope visibly or feel uneven
  • Cracks in interior drywall at corners of windows and doors
  • Bowing or leaning basement walls

Not all cracks are serious — hairline vertical cracks in poured concrete are often just settling and may need only minor sealing. Horizontal or diagonal cracks, or cracks wider than 1/4 inch, warrant professional evaluation immediately.

🔥 Fire & Smoke Damage Questions

Can I stay in my house after a fire?

Whether a home is habitable after a fire depends on the extent of damage. Do NOT re-enter until the fire department has cleared the structure as safe. Hazards include: structural instability, electrical hazards from water damage, toxic smoke residue, and carbon monoxide. Even a small fire can produce smoke damage and soot throughout the entire home via HVAC systems.

For any significant fire: (1) Do not turn on HVAC — it spreads soot. (2) Do not eat food that was in the home. (3) Do not clean soot yourself — improper cleaning permanently embeds it into surfaces. (4) Call a professional fire restoration company within 24 hours. (5) Contact your insurance company immediately.

📋 Insurance & Claims Questions

Does homeowner insurance cover water damage?

Typically COVERED:

  • Burst pipes (sudden and accidental)
  • Washing machine or dishwasher overflow
  • Water heater failure (sudden burst)
  • Rain water intrusion from storm-damaged roof
  • Ice dam damage

Typically NOT COVERED:

  • Flooding from outside (requires separate NFIP flood insurance)
  • Gradual leaks you knew about or should have known about
  • Sewer or drain backup (requires a separate rider/endorsement)
  • Lack of maintenance (e.g., aging pipes you neglected)
  • Groundwater seepage

Always document damage immediately with photos and video. File your claim within 24–48 hours of discovery. Ask your adjuster specifically about "additional living expense" (ALE) coverage if you need to temporarily relocate.

Do I need my own contractor or will insurance send one?

You have the right to choose your own contractor for insurance work in all 50 states. Insurance companies may suggest or refer a "preferred contractor" — these are vendors who have a business relationship with the insurer, which may or may not benefit you.

Best practice: (1) Get the insurance adjuster's assessment in writing. (2) Get 2–3 independent estimates from licensed contractors. (3) If estimates exceed the adjuster's allowance, ask the contractor to "supplement" with the insurance company — most large restoration companies do this routinely. (4) Never sign an Assignment of Benefits (AOB) form without consulting a public adjuster or attorney — this transfers your claim rights to the contractor.

📜 Licensing & Credentials

How do I verify a contractor's license by state?

Each state maintains its own contractor licensing database. To verify:

  1. Ask the contractor for their license number and the issuing state
  2. Search your state's contractor licensing board website (search "[state name] contractor license lookup")
  3. Confirm: license is active, not expired or suspended, license type matches work being performed, no disciplinary actions

Major state licensing boards: Florida DBPR (myfloridalicense.com), California CSLB (cslb.ca.gov), Texas TDLR (tdlr.texas.gov), New York DOS (dos.ny.gov), Georgia SWCB/GREC. Most state boards have instant online lookup tools.

What certifications should a water damage restoration company have?
  • IICRC Water Restoration Technician (WRT): Industry standard — required for proper drying protocols
  • IICRC Applied Structural Drying (ASD): Advanced certification for complex drying projects
  • IICRC Applied Microbial Remediation Technician (AMRT): Required for mold work combined with water damage
  • State contractor license: Varies by state — required for reconstruction work
  • General liability insurance: Minimum $1M per occurrence recommended
  • Workers' compensation insurance: Mandatory in most states with any employees
