HVAC Services

Explore the types of HVAC services available through our directory

AC Repair

Emergency and scheduled air conditioning repair for all brands and systems. Common issues include refrigerant leaks, compressor failure, frozen coils, thermostat problems, and capacitor replacement.

Heating Repair

Furnace, boiler, and heat pump repair for gas, electric, and oil systems. Covers ignitor failure, blower motor issues, heat exchanger cracks, pilot light problems, and thermostat malfunctions.

Duct Cleaning

Professional duct cleaning removes dust, allergens, mold spores, and debris from your HVAC ductwork. Recommended every 3-5 years or after renovations, pest infestations, or visible mold growth.

AC Installation

New central air conditioning installation includes load calculation, equipment selection, ductwork evaluation, and professional installation. Proper sizing is critical - oversized or undersized units waste energy and fail prematurely.

Furnace Installation

Gas and electric furnace installation with proper sizing, venting, and code compliance. Modern high-efficiency furnaces (95%+ AFUE) can significantly reduce heating costs compared to older 80% AFUE units.

Mini-Split Systems

Ductless mini-split installation for room additions, garages, sunrooms, and homes without ductwork. Multi-zone systems can heat and cool individual rooms independently for maximum efficiency and comfort.

Air Quality

Indoor air quality solutions include whole-home air purifiers, UV germicidal lights, humidifiers, dehumidifiers, ERV/HRV ventilation systems, and HEPA filtration upgrades for allergy and asthma relief.

Duct Sealing

Leaky ductwork can waste 20-30% of conditioned air. Professional duct sealing uses mastic sealant and aerosol-based systems to close gaps and improve efficiency, comfort, and air quality throughout your home.

HVAC FAQ

How often should I have my HVAC system serviced?
Your HVAC system should be professionally serviced twice per year - once in spring for the cooling system and once in fall for the heating system. Regular maintenance extends equipment life by 5-10 years, prevents breakdowns, maintains warranty coverage, and keeps your system running at peak efficiency.
How long does an HVAC system typically last?
A well-maintained central air conditioner typically lasts 15-20 years. Gas furnaces last 15-25 years. Heat pumps average 12-15 years. Ductless mini-splits last 15-20 years. If your system is approaching these ages and requiring frequent repairs, replacement is usually more cost-effective than continued repair.
Why is my AC running but not cooling the house?
Common causes include low refrigerant (from a leak), a dirty or frozen evaporator coil, a failed compressor, a clogged air filter restricting airflow, a malfunctioning thermostat, or a bad capacitor. Start by checking your air filter and thermostat settings. If those are fine, call a licensed HVAC technician - refrigerant issues and electrical problems require professional diagnosis.
What size HVAC system do I need for my home?
Proper HVAC sizing requires a Manual J load calculation that considers your home's square footage, insulation levels, window count and orientation, climate zone, and ductwork condition. A general rule is 1 ton of cooling per 500-600 sq ft, but this varies widely. An oversized system short-cycles (wastes energy, fails sooner) while an undersized unit runs constantly and never reaches comfort levels.
Is it worth upgrading to a high-efficiency HVAC system?
High-efficiency systems (16+ SEER AC, 95%+ AFUE furnace) cost 20-40% more upfront but can reduce energy bills by 30-50% compared to older units. Federal tax credits and utility rebates can offset the cost difference. If your current system is over 10 years old and you plan to stay in your home, the upgrade typically pays for itself within 5-7 years.
