Water Damage Services

Explore the types of water damage restoration services available

Water Removal

Emergency water extraction using truck-mounted pumps and industrial extractors. Rapid removal of standing water from floods, burst pipes, appliance failures, and sewage backups to prevent secondary damage.

Flood Cleanup

Comprehensive flood cleanup includes water extraction, debris removal, sanitization, and antimicrobial treatment. Flood water is classified by contamination level, with Category 3 (black water) requiring specialized handling.

Basement Flooding

Basement-specific restoration addresses sump pump failures, foundation seepage, and groundwater intrusion. Includes water removal, dehumidification, mold prevention, and recommendations for waterproofing to prevent recurrence.

Content Restoration

Specialized cleaning and restoration of personal belongings, furniture, electronics, documents, and photos damaged by water. Techniques include ultrasonic cleaning, freeze-drying, and ozone treatment.

Structural Drying

Industrial dehumidifiers and air movers dry out walls, floors, ceilings, and structural framing. Proper structural drying prevents mold growth, wood rot, and long-term damage. Monitored with moisture meters until targets are met.

Moisture Detection

Thermal imaging cameras and pin/pinless moisture meters locate hidden water behind walls, under floors, and in ceiling cavities. Essential for mapping the full extent of water intrusion before restoration begins.

Water Damage FAQ

How quickly should I call a water damage restoration company?
Call immediately. Mold can begin growing within 24-48 hours of water exposure, and structural damage worsens rapidly. Most reputable restoration companies offer 60-minute response times for emergencies. The faster extraction and drying begins, the less damage and lower cost you will face.
Does homeowner's insurance cover water damage restoration?
Most homeowner's insurance policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as burst pipes or appliance failures. Flood damage from natural events typically requires a separate flood insurance policy through FEMA's National Flood Insurance Program. Gradual damage from neglected maintenance is usually not covered. Document everything with photos and contact your insurer immediately.
What is the difference between Category 1, 2, and 3 water damage?
Category 1 (clean water) comes from supply lines, faucets, or rainwater and poses minimal health risk. Category 2 (gray water) contains contaminants from dishwashers, washing machines, or sump pumps and can cause illness. Category 3 (black water) includes sewage, floodwater, and toilet overflow - it contains serious pathogens and requires full PPE and specialized protocols.
How long does the water damage restoration process take?
Water extraction typically takes 1-2 days. Structural drying takes 3-5 days depending on the extent of saturation, building materials, and humidity levels. Full restoration including repairs, painting, and flooring replacement can take 1-4 weeks. Your restoration company should provide daily moisture readings and a clear timeline.
Can I stay in my home during water damage restoration?
For minor water damage (a single room, clean water), you can often stay home during restoration. However, if the damage involves sewage, extensive flooding across multiple rooms, or if the HVAC system is compromised, temporary relocation is recommended. Your insurance may cover additional living expenses (ALE) during displacement.
