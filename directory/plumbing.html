Plumbing Services

Explore the types of plumbing services available through our directory

Emergency Plumber

24/7 response for burst pipes, major leaks, overflowing toilets, and gas line emergencies. Emergency plumbers arrive fast to minimize water damage and restore service.

Drain Cleaning

Professional drain cleaning uses hydro-jetting, snaking, and camera inspection to clear stubborn clogs in kitchen sinks, shower drains, floor drains, and main sewer lines.

Pipe Repair

From pinhole leaks to corroded galvanized pipes, professional pipe repair includes patching, joint replacement, and trenchless pipe lining to fix problems without tearing out walls.

Sewer Line

Sewer line services include camera inspections, root removal, trenchless sewer repair, and full sewer line replacement. Critical for preventing sewage backups into your home.

Water Heater

Installation, repair, and replacement of tank and tankless water heaters. Includes sediment flushing, anode rod replacement, thermostat repair, and emergency no-hot-water calls.

Repiping

Whole-house repiping replaces aging galvanized, polybutylene, or lead pipes with modern copper or PEX. Essential for homes with recurring leaks, low pressure, or discolored water.

Leak Detection

Advanced leak detection uses thermal imaging, acoustic sensors, and pressure testing to find hidden leaks in walls, slabs, and underground pipes without unnecessary demolition.

Gas Line

Licensed gas line plumbers handle installation, repair, leak detection, and appliance hookups for natural gas and propane. Gas work requires specialized licensing in most states.

Plumbing FAQ

How much does an emergency plumber cost?
Emergency plumber rates typically range from $150 to $500 for the initial visit, depending on the time of day and severity. After-hours and weekend calls often carry a premium. The total cost depends on the repair needed - a simple fix may stay under $300, while a burst pipe requiring drywall access could run $1,000 or more.
How do I know if I need a licensed plumber or can DIY?
Minor tasks like replacing a faucet aerator or unclogging a sink with a plunger are safe DIY jobs. However, anything involving water supply lines, gas lines, sewer connections, water heater installation, or work behind walls should be handled by a licensed plumber. Most jurisdictions require permits for significant plumbing work, and DIY jobs can void your homeowner's insurance.
What should I do if a pipe bursts in my home?
Immediately shut off your main water supply valve (usually near the water meter or where the main line enters your home). Open faucets to drain remaining water. Turn off electricity in affected areas if water is near outlets or panels. Call an emergency plumber right away, and start moving valuables away from the water. Document damage with photos for your insurance claim.
How often should drains be professionally cleaned?
Most homes benefit from professional drain cleaning every 1-2 years as preventive maintenance. If you have older pipes, large trees near your sewer line, or frequently deal with slow drains, annual cleaning is recommended. Commercial kitchens and restaurants typically need quarterly service due to grease buildup.
Should I repair or replace my water heater?
If your water heater is less than 8 years old and the issue is a faulty thermostat, heating element, or pressure valve, repair is usually the better choice. If the tank is leaking, the unit is over 10 years old, or you are facing repeated repairs, replacement is more cost-effective. Upgrading to a tankless unit can save 20-30% on water heating costs.
