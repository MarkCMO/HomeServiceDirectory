Sewage & Biohazard Services

Explore the types of biohazard cleanup services available through our directory

Sewage Cleanup

Emergency sewage backup cleanup includes extraction, contaminated material removal, antimicrobial treatment, and structural drying. Sewage is Category 3 (black water) containing bacteria, viruses, and parasites - professional PPE and disposal protocols are mandatory.

Biohazard Remediation

Professional cleanup of blood, bodily fluids, and other biohazardous materials following accidents, unattended deaths, or medical events. Technicians follow OSHA bloodborne pathogen standards and dispose of materials through regulated medical waste channels.

Septic Overflow

Septic system failures and overflows require immediate professional cleanup to prevent groundwater contamination and health hazards. Services include waste extraction, affected soil removal, sanitization, and coordination with septic service companies for system repair.

Contamination Cleanup

Decontamination of properties affected by chemical spills, drug lab residue (methamphetamine), tear gas, and industrial contaminants. Requires specialized testing, cleaning agents, and verification sampling to ensure the property meets habitable standards.

Hoarding Cleanup

Compassionate, discreet hoarding cleanup includes sorting, removal, deep cleaning, sanitization, and biohazard disposal when needed. Teams work with property owners respectfully to restore livable conditions while preserving items of value and importance.

Crime Scene Cleanup

Professional crime scene and trauma cleanup after law enforcement releases the scene. Technicians remove all biohazardous materials, decontaminate surfaces, eliminate odors, and restore the property. Families should never attempt crime scene cleanup themselves.

Sewage & Biohazard FAQ

Is it safe to clean up sewage myself?
No. Sewage contains dangerous bacteria (E. coli, Salmonella), viruses (Hepatitis A, Norovirus), and parasites that can cause serious illness through skin contact, ingestion, or inhalation. Professional cleanup requires full PPE including respirators, Tyvek suits, and rubber boots. All contaminated porous materials (carpet, padding, drywall below the flood line) must be removed and disposed of properly.
How much does sewage cleanup cost?
Sewage cleanup costs range from $2,000-$10,000 for a residential basement backup, depending on the area affected and materials contaminated. Small, contained sewage events may cost $1,000-$3,000. Large-scale sewage flooding with significant material removal and reconstruction can reach $15,000-$25,000. Your homeowner's insurance may cover sewage backup if you have the optional sewer/drain endorsement.
Does insurance cover biohazard cleanup?
Coverage depends on the cause and your policy. Sewage backup may be covered if you have the sewer/drain backup endorsement (typically $40-$75/year add-on). Crime scene and trauma cleanup may be covered under your liability or dwelling coverage. Many states have victim compensation programs that cover crime scene cleanup costs. Hoarding cleanup is generally not covered by insurance.
How quickly should sewage be cleaned up?
Sewage cleanup should begin within hours, not days. After 24 hours, bacteria multiply rapidly and contamination spreads to surrounding materials. After 48 hours, mold begins to colonize wet surfaces. The longer sewage sits, the more material must be removed and the higher the cost. Most professional sewage cleanup companies offer 1-2 hour emergency response times.
Who is responsible for sewer line backups?
Homeowners are responsible for the sewer lateral (the pipe from your house to the municipal main). The city or municipality maintains the main sewer line. If a backup is caused by a city main line blockage, the municipality may be liable. If the backup is in your lateral (due to tree roots, grease, or pipe collapse), it is your responsibility. A plumber can camera the line to determine the blockage location.
