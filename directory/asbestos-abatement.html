Abatement & Environmental Services

Explore the types of hazardous material services available through our directory

Asbestos Removal

Licensed asbestos abatement includes containment, HEPA filtration, wet removal techniques, proper bagging, and disposal at certified landfills. Common asbestos locations include floor tile, pipe insulation, popcorn ceilings, roofing materials, and siding in pre-1980 homes.

Lead Paint Abatement

EPA RRP-certified lead paint removal for homes built before 1978. Methods include chemical stripping, encapsulation, component replacement, and HEPA-assisted scraping. Especially critical in homes with children, as lead dust causes developmental harm at any exposure level.

Radon Mitigation

Radon mitigation systems reduce indoor radon levels below the EPA action level of 4 pCi/L. Sub-slab depressurization is the most common method, using a vent pipe and fan to draw radon from beneath the foundation and exhaust it safely above the roofline.

Environmental Testing

Professional testing for asbestos, lead paint, radon, mold, VOCs, and other environmental hazards. Samples are analyzed by accredited laboratories with certified chain-of-custody procedures. Testing is essential before renovation of older homes and for real estate transactions.

Hazmat Services

Specialized hazardous material handling for chemical spills, contaminated soil, underground storage tank removal, and industrial decontamination. Hazmat teams follow EPA, OSHA, and DOT regulations for containment, cleanup, transportation, and disposal of regulated materials.

Air Monitoring

Continuous air monitoring during abatement projects ensures worker safety and prevents fiber release to occupied areas. Post-abatement clearance air sampling confirms that asbestos fiber counts are below EPA clearance levels before containment is removed and the space is reoccupied.

Asbestos & Lead FAQ

How do I know if my home has asbestos?
You cannot identify asbestos by looking at a material - laboratory analysis is required. If your home was built before 1980, assume any insulation, floor tile, ceiling texture, pipe wrap, or roofing material may contain asbestos until tested. Never disturb suspected asbestos materials. Hire a certified asbestos inspector to collect samples using proper containment techniques and send them to an accredited lab.
How much does asbestos removal cost?
Asbestos removal costs $15-$75 per square foot depending on the material type, location, and accessibility. Removing asbestos pipe insulation from a basement may cost $1,500-$3,000. Popcorn ceiling removal from a standard home runs $3,000-$8,000. Full abatement of a heavily contaminated home can exceed $20,000. Testing alone costs $200-$800. Always get multiple bids from licensed abatement contractors.
Is lead paint only a concern in old homes?
Lead-based paint was banned for residential use in 1978, so any home built before that year may contain lead paint. The older the home, the more likely and higher the lead content. Lead paint in good condition (not peeling, chipping, or chalking) on non-friction surfaces may be safely left in place or encapsulated. However, any renovation that disturbs painted surfaces in pre-1978 homes must follow EPA RRP rules.
What radon level is dangerous?
The EPA recommends taking action at 4 pCi/L (picocuries per liter) or above, though there is no truly "safe" level of radon. The WHO recommends action at 2.7 pCi/L. Radon is the second leading cause of lung cancer after smoking. Every home should be tested - radon levels vary dramatically even between neighboring houses. Short-term test kits cost $15-$30, or hire a professional for continuous monitoring.
Do I need abatement before renovating an older home?
Yes. Federal EPA RRP (Renovation, Repair, and Painting) rules require that any contractor working on pre-1978 homes be EPA-certified and follow lead-safe work practices. If asbestos-containing materials will be disturbed during renovation, they must be removed by a licensed abatement contractor before renovation work begins. Failure to test and abate before renovation creates serious health hazards and significant legal liability.
