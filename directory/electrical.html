Electrical Services

Explore the types of electrical services available through our directory

Emergency Electrician

24/7 emergency response for power outages, sparking outlets, burning smells, tripped breakers that will not reset, exposed wiring, and electrical fires. Emergency electricians diagnose and resolve dangerous conditions quickly to restore safety.

Panel Upgrade

Electrical panel upgrades from 100-amp to 200-amp or 400-amp service to support modern demand. Essential when adding EV chargers, hot tubs, workshops, or if your home still has a fuse box or Federal Pacific/Zinsco panel.

Wiring Repair

Repair and replacement of damaged, outdated, or hazardous wiring. Includes knob-and-tube replacement, aluminum wiring remediation, GFCI/AFCI upgrades, and rewiring for homes with cloth-insulated or deteriorated wiring.

Outlet Installation

Installation of standard outlets, GFCI outlets for wet areas, 240V outlets for appliances, USB outlets, tamper-resistant receptacles, and outdoor weather-proof outlets. Includes proper circuit load evaluation.

Generator Installation

Whole-home standby generator installation with automatic transfer switch, concrete pad, gas line connection, and load management. Portable generator hookup with manual transfer switch and inlet box for safe backfeeding.

EV Charger Installation

Level 2 EV charger installation (240V, 40-50 amp circuit) in garages or driveways. Includes panel capacity assessment, dedicated circuit run, NEMA 14-50 outlet or hardwired charger mounting, and permit coordination.

Lighting

Interior and exterior lighting installation including recessed lighting, ceiling fans, chandeliers, under-cabinet lighting, landscape lighting, security floodlights, and smart lighting systems with dimmer controls.

Surge Protection

Whole-home surge protection installed at the electrical panel guards all circuits against lightning strikes and power surges. Protects HVAC systems, appliances, electronics, and smart home devices from voltage spikes that cause premature failure.

Electrical FAQ

When should I call an emergency electrician?
Call an emergency electrician immediately if you smell burning from an outlet or panel, see sparking, have a breaker that trips repeatedly and will not stay reset, notice discolored or warm wall plates, experience a partial power outage in your home, or if any outlet or switch shocks you. These are signs of serious electrical hazards that can cause fires.
How do I know if my electrical panel needs to be upgraded?
Signs you need a panel upgrade include frequently tripping breakers, a fuse box instead of a circuit breaker panel, a Federal Pacific or Zinsco panel (known fire hazards), 100-amp service with plans to add major loads (EV charger, hot tub, pool), visible corrosion or burn marks, and insufficient space for new circuits. Most modern homes need at least 200-amp service.
How much does it cost to rewire a house?
Whole-house rewiring typically costs $8,000-$30,000 depending on home size, accessibility (single story vs. multi-story), wall construction, and local labor rates. A 1,500 sq ft ranch may cost $8,000-$12,000. A 3,000 sq ft two-story home could run $15,000-$25,000. Rewiring usually includes a panel upgrade, new outlets, and bringing everything up to current code.
Do I need a permit for electrical work?
Most jurisdictions require permits for any electrical work beyond simple like-for-like replacements (swapping a light switch or outlet). Panel upgrades, new circuits, rewiring, generator installation, EV charger circuits, and any work that changes your electrical system require permits and inspections. A licensed electrician will pull the permit and schedule inspections as part of the job.
What is the difference between GFCI and AFCI protection?
GFCI (Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter) protects against electrocution by detecting current leakage to ground - required in bathrooms, kitchens, garages, outdoors, and near water. AFCI (Arc Fault Circuit Interrupter) protects against electrical fires by detecting dangerous arcing in wiring - required in bedrooms, living rooms, and most living spaces. Modern code often requires both types of protection.
