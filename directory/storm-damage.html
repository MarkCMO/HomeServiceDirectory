Storm Damage Services

Explore the types of storm restoration services available through our directory

Storm Cleanup

Comprehensive storm damage assessment and cleanup after severe weather events. Teams address structural damage, water intrusion, fallen trees, and debris removal while coordinating with insurance adjusters to document the full scope of damage.

Wind Damage

Wind damage repair for siding, roofing, windows, fencing, and structural components. High winds can peel back shingles, blow off siding, shatter windows, and compromise structural integrity. Professional assessment determines visible and hidden damage.

Hail Damage

Hail damage inspection and repair for roofing, siding, gutters, windows, and outdoor equipment. Hail damage is often invisible from the ground but causes granule loss on shingles, dents in metal, and cracks in vinyl that lead to premature failure.

Hurricane Cleanup

Full-scale hurricane damage restoration including water extraction, structural stabilization, roof repair or replacement, mold prevention, and complete property reconstruction. Experienced crews mobilize to hurricane-affected areas to provide rapid recovery services.

Emergency Tarping

Immediate roof tarping and board-up services to prevent further water damage after storm events. Heavy-duty tarps are secured over damaged roof areas, and broken windows and doors are boarded to protect the interior from weather and intrusion until permanent repairs begin.

Tree Removal

Emergency tree removal for trees fallen on homes, vehicles, power lines, and roadways. Certified arborists and tree service crews safely cut, section, and remove downed trees while protecting the underlying structure from additional damage during removal.

Debris Removal

Large-scale debris clearing after storms, tornadoes, and hurricanes. Services include dumpster and roll-off container placement, heavy equipment operation for large debris, sorting of salvageable materials, and proper disposal at approved facilities.

Storm Damage FAQ

What should I do immediately after storm damage?
First, ensure everyone is safe and stay away from downed power lines and unstable structures. Document all damage with photos and video before any cleanup. Contact your insurance company to start a claim. Call a storm damage restoration company for emergency tarping or board-up to prevent further damage. Keep all receipts for emergency expenses. Do not make permanent repairs until your adjuster has inspected the damage.
Does insurance cover storm damage?
Most homeowner's policies cover wind and hail damage under the dwelling and personal property sections. However, flood damage from storm surge or rising water requires separate flood insurance. Many policies in coastal and hail-prone areas have separate wind/hail deductibles (1-5% of home value) instead of the standard flat deductible. Check your policy's specific deductible structure before filing.
How do I avoid storm chaser scams after a major storm?
After major storms, unlicensed contractors canvass neighborhoods offering quick repairs. Protect yourself by verifying the contractor is licensed and insured in your state, never paying more than 10-15% upfront, getting a written contract with scope and price before work begins, avoiding anyone who asks you to sign over your insurance claim rights (Assignment of Benefits), and checking reviews and references. Legitimate contractors do not need to knock on your door.
How long do I have to file a storm damage insurance claim?
Most policies require you to report damage "promptly" or "as soon as practicable." In practice, you should file within days of the storm, not weeks or months. Many states have statutes of limitations of 1-2 years for property damage claims. However, hail damage discovered later during a roof inspection may still be covered if you can prove it occurred during a specific storm event. File as soon as you discover the damage.
Who pays for tree removal after a storm?
If a tree falls on your home or other insured structure, your homeowner's insurance typically covers removal (usually $500-$1,000 per tree, with a total limit). If a tree falls in your yard but does not hit a structure, most policies do not cover removal - that is your expense ($300-$2,000 depending on size and access). If a neighbor's tree falls on your property, your insurance handles it, not your neighbor's, regardless of whose tree it was.
